# Eval v3: gold-label benchmark (plan, 2026-10-13)

Benchmarks v1 and v2 were shaped by GLM: it filtered which prompts were kept (19% of gold-labelled candidates
dropped, most of them subtle attacks: prompt_shield kept 37 of 81 block prompts, deepset 39 of 60) and it generated
the hard benign emails. A model trained on GLM-verified data agrees with that selection, so v1/v2 numbers are
optimistic and tilted against Jev. They stay valid for comparing our runs with each other, not as final results.

## Rules

1. Labels come only from human annotation or from how a dataset was built. No model filters prompts, no
   model-generated prompts, no LLM-labelled datasets.
2. Each dataset is kept or dropped from its documentation (card or paper), with the source recorded below.
3. Label noise is handled by people: GLM disagreements only rank which prompts are reviewed first; a sample of
   about 150 is reviewed by the team to estimate noise per dataset, then fixed or excluded by a person.
4. The headline score uses datasets never trained on; test splits of training datasets are reported separately.
5. The eval is frozen and never used to choose checkpoints or thresholds. A separate dev set, also with
   unfiltered gold labels, does that.
6. Results per group with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals; the headline is the average across groups.

## Size and gaps

From existing candidates (`data/benchmark_candidates.jsonl`), the kept datasets give 3,597 prompts (1,902 block,
1,695 allow). Two groups are too thin:

- direct injection: 172 attacks, 56 benign (deepset only);
- indirect injection: the only benign text is Enron email, so style alone separates it from attacks.

Both need gold sets that pair the same text with and without an injection. Candidate: Microsoft BIPIA (benign
emails, tables and code, with and without injected instructions); license and format to check.

## One-time cost, after the dataset list is approved

- Jev and Bedrock on the prompts they lack (256 Jev, 286 Bedrock, plus any new pairs): under $0.50.
- GPU: one scoring pass of the existing models, about 20 minutes. No retraining until this eval is frozen.
- Team review of about 150 prompts.
